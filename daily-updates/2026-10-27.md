6. **Actionable insight for our projects** — `tiny-mcp-audit` has 26 rules (MCP000–MCP073) pinned to spec `2026-07-28`. Three concrete gaps:
   - **Server Cards are a new pre-connection attack surface.** Nothing in MCP002–MCP004 (server identity/provenance) validates a `server-card` or `/.well-known/ai-catalog.json`. A malicious card can advertise fake transports before a client ever connects. → candidate rule `MCP005` (card/transport provenance) or `MCP074`.
   - **Token audience binding.** The TypeScript SDK's new `expectedResource` exists because the SDK's bearer auth *accepted tokens issued for a different resource server* (GHSA-w4fh-qvv9-3v23; mcp 1.10.0–1.29.1 and 2.0.0–2.1.1). Our audit should flag a server that does not bind token audience to the resource. → candidate `MCP023`.
   - **The lifecycle/leak pain cluster is our strongest product gap.** Codex per-thread MCP process explosion (353 processes / 9.6 GiB, issue #53285) and Gemini CLI's >128-tool surfaces are the two loudest unreliability classes across all seven agent CLIs. We already ship `tiny-mcp-client` / `tiny-mcp-observability` / `tiny-mcp-skel` — a **connection-reuse + reclamation manager** (one server process, bounded fan-out, per-thread reuse, explicit teardown) is the natural next repo in the family and it is directly demanded by the market.
